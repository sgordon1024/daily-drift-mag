Science / engineering

Pink glow. Hard numbers.

Hampton, Virginia

At NASA Langley in Hampton, Virginia, a test wing glowed pink-purple under ultraviolet light. The color had a job: high-speed cameras tracked changes in pressure-sensitive paint to measure airflow pressure. NASA calls it the first use of this technique on a large-scale, freely moving model in a low-oxygen environment. The Benchmark Supercritical Wing is a reference model, not a particular aircraft. Its measurements help sharpen computer simulations. The glow gets your attention. The data is the point.

Science / engineering

Six flights. Then maintenance.

United States

NASA’s X-59 logged six flights on September 22, 24 and 25—two a day, two more than its previous weekly record. NASA reported the tally on October 2. The aircraft had reached 38 test flights and Mach 1.53, above its planned Mach 1.4 cruise. Engineers were expanding the tested range of speeds, altitudes and maneuvers. Next: roughly three weeks of planned maintenance, then a systems review. Flight testing includes the part where you stop flying and check the machine.

Unidentified does not mean extraterrestrial.

EVERGREEN FIELD GUIDE

Unidentified is a question, not an answer.

UAP means “unidentified anomalous phenomena.” It describes an observation that has not been explained; it does not establish what caused it. NASA says many sightings lack the high-quality data needed for scientific conclusions, and it has found no evidence that UAP are extraterrestrial. A witness account records what someone reports, not proof of an explanation. Keep the observation, proposed explanations and confirmed findings in separate boxes. NASA’s 2023 independent study addressed better ways to collect and analyze data, rather than reviewing past incidents. Stay curious. Bring better receipts.
