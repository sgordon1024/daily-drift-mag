UFO / UAP / ARCHIVE CASE FILE

The memo recorded a claim, then stopped.

A one-page FBI memorandum carried a second- or third-hand account into the files. It did not carry an investigation with it.

Washington, D.C. · Memo dated March 22, 1950
FBI account · March 25, 2013
Daily Drift feature added October 11, 2026 · approximately 2 minutes

On March 22, 1950, Guy Hottel, the FBI special agent in charge in Washington, sent headquarters a one-page memorandum. It relayed what an informant said an Air Force investigator had said: three circular craft had been recovered in New Mexico, each carrying three small human-shaped bodies.

The chain of custody ends inside that sentence. The memo names no investigator, recovery team, location or physical evidence. Its final line says no further evaluation was attempted.

The FBI says it never investigated the claim. It also says its files do not verify the theory that the account repeated a contemporary hoax. The Bureau dates the memo nearly three years after the Roswell events and says there is no reason to connect the two.

The page was not a later disclosure of a hidden investigation. According to the FBI, it was released publicly in the late 1970s and had appeared on the Bureau’s website before the FBI Vault launched in 2011. Online retellings gave the document a second life that its original paper trail did not support.

What the record can support

The memorandum is an authentic FBI record of an unverified report. It is not proof that a recovery occurred. Evidence that could change the assessment would include named sources, contemporaneous recovery records, material remains or an independently documented investigation.
